As AI instructional agents become more prevalent in education, a fundamental question arises: can they foster student engagement as effectively as human teachers? This AERA paper presents an empirical comparison of AI instructional agents and human teachers using multimodal neurophysiological measurements.

Students learned with either an AI agent or a human teacher while their engagement was tracked through EEG, physiological signals, and behavioral measures. The study compares engagement levels, attentional dynamics, and emotional responses between conditions, and examines which dimensions of engagement AI can and cannot foster. Results reveal nuanced differences: AI agents maintain comparable cognitive engagement in some respects but show limitations in socio-emotional engagement. The findings inform the design of AI teaching systems and clarify the complementary roles of human and AI instructors.
