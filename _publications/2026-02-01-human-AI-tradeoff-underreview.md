Collaborating with AI may improve task efficiency, but at what cost to social connection? This randomized controlled study investigates the trade-off between cognitive efficiency and social connection in human-human versus human-AI collaboration.

Participants completed collaborative problem-solving tasks with either a human or an AI partner, with cognitive performance and perceived social connection measured across sessions. The results reveal a systematic trade-off: AI partners enhance cognitive efficiency—faster completion, better performance on well-defined tasks—but significantly reduce perceived social connection, emotional engagement, and sense of shared understanding. The paper further tracks how this trade-off evolves over time and identifies individual differences in susceptibility. The findings carry important implications for designing collaborative learning environments and for understanding the social dimensions of human-AI interaction.
