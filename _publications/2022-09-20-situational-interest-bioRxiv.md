Situational interest plays a critical role in classroom learning, but most studies rely on a single paradigm and cannot capture its dynamic nature. This preprint examines the role of situational interest in classroom learning by combining laboratory and naturalistic paradigms.

Across controlled experiments and real classroom observations, the study models how interest fluctuates over learning episodes and examines its physiological correlates using multimodal signals. The results show that situational interest is highly dynamic, sensitive to instructional events, and predictive of learning outcomes and engagement. The dual-paradigm design provides converging evidence for the mechanisms of interest and demonstrates the value of physiological measures in interest research. The findings support interest-based adaptive instruction and deepen understanding of motivational dynamics in education.
