Do teachers see students' classroom experiences the same way students do? This working paper examines the alignment between teachers' and students' representations of subjective classroom experiences.

Using paired teacher-student reports and multimodal classroom data, the study compares how teachers perceive students' cognitive and emotional states with students' own reported experiences. Results reveal systematic gaps: teachers tend to overestimate or underestimate specific aspects of students' in-class experiences, and the degree of alignment varies across content and student characteristics. The findings have important implications for teacher practice, student-centered assessment, and the design of feedback systems that bridge teacher and student perspectives.
